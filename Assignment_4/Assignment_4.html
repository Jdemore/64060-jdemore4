#same checks as the plots in one table, plus smallest cluster size
kStats <- function(k, data) {
  set.seed(123)
  km <- kmeans(data, centers = k, nstart = 25)
  sil <- cluster::silhouette(km$cluster, dist(data))
  c(k = k,
    wss = round(km$tot.withinss, 1),
    betweenTotal = round(km$betweenss / km$totss, 3), #share of variation between clusters
    avgSil = round(mean(sil[, "sil_width"]), 3),
    smallest = min(km$size))
}

#kcca has no nstart, so run 25 seeds and keep the one w/ the lowest total distance

  set.seed(seed)

Part A - Reasoning

Weights were left the same - no mention was made of these values holding more or less
importance than any other values. 
Z-score was used to normalize the scale since Euclidean distance is scale dependent.
Market cap would have otherwise decided everything.
Though weights weren't added manually, Euclidean distance ignores correlation, so
size/profitability end up acting as though they have a higher weight. 
ROE, ROA, Market Cap seem very correlated from the data/process used here.
I also ran Manhattan distance (following our class lectures / second method may be better)
- keeping the same lack of weights. 
Size&Profitability would be the main ways these are determined anyway.
Algo: kmeans w/ Euclidean distance; the 9 vars are all continuious numbers, with
the goal being to split firms into non overlapping groups (thus why we used kmeans)
nstart=25 (from slides, but runs 25 times from random starts) with static seed set.
Also ran against 50 other seeds to verify the result would be the same (for thoroughness)
Manhattan distance showed the 11 major firms came out the same, with 3 minor ones moving.
I maintained Euclidean distance for the assignment since it's more stable.
Outliers: Allergan, Chattem
Both are >3 SD from mean on one vars.
I chose K=3 for my clustering because the silhouette peaks at k=3, and the elbow
plot had the larger drops between 1-2 and 2-3. K=2 would have been too small to have
meaningful clusters, so I went with 3. I also tried running this with k=5, but some
of the groups weren't visually meaningful, so I kept k=3 to maintain the clarity.
Our data set is rather small, so this made sense to me. More technically, I would
say that k=5 made the clusters weaker, and also to note; they fell apart under manhattan.
Quality: Avg silhouette .280; clusters are separated but not too sharply. This made
sense with our 21 firm & no obvious data gaps. Loosest cluster was AGN-AHM-BAY-PHA (.136)
and AHM is negative; so it's closer to other clusters than its own (technically)

From reading hte clusters off the centroids, Z score of 0 represents industry average.
Positive is above; negative is below. clProfile used for the actual numbers.
Large profitable leaders {ABT, AZN, BMY, GSK, JNJ, LLY, MRK, NVS, PFE, SGP, WYE}
Biggest Firms (by market cap), avg of 97.1b vs 57.7b industry average. All 4 of the
big pharma giants are in this group. 
Best profitability ROE 35.7% (avg 25.8), ROA 15.0% (avg 10.5), net margin 20.2 (avg 15.7)
Highest asset turnover (.80), lowest risk (.43), lowest leverage (.33).
These have weaker growth (likely because companies of this size lose room to scale;
i'd equate this to something similar to a population curve from biology), also indicates
steady income for these companies.

Overvalued Underperformers (AGN, AHM, BAY, PHA) 
Highest PE 46.9 vs 25.5 average (Allergan&Pharmacia leading here at 82.5 and 56.5 respectively)
Worst profitability; lowest ROE (11.3%) and ROA (5.1%) with net margin 6.7%
Lower rev growth than the Large Profitable Leaders group also. This shows that
investors pay a lot per dollar earned within this group, price === expectation, not performance.
AHM doesn't fit great here (20.7) with k=3, would likely be separated for k=5. loosest cluster.

High-growth Risky Small caps (AVE, CHTT, ELN, IVX, MRX, WPI)
smallest firms with avg mcap of 9.2b, fastest growing rev growth 23.5% vs. 13.4% avg
Riskiest - leverage 1.25 vs .59 average & highest beta .65
